# Research log

Published findings that bear on the long road. Each entry is one sentence with the authors and a link. An entry is added only when the source is peer-reviewed or comparably authoritative and the result changes what we know about living beyond Earth.

## Food beyond Earth

- **2026-11-19.** David Handy, Aymeric Goyer and colleagues grew potatoes in five lunar regolith simulants and found that plain simulant severely stunts the plants, while mixing in 30% vermicompost restores growth to the level of ordinary soil. [npj Microgravity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-026-00668-z)
- **2026-11-05.** Nagarajan Bharathy, Saiyyeda Firdous and Theivasigamani Parthasarathi conclude in a review that plants germinate in lunar regolith readily, and that the real barrier is keeping them reproducing with stable genomes across generations, which no intervention has yet solved. [Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspas.2026.1953450)
- **2026-04-17.** Jéssica Carneiro Oliveira, Rafael Loureiro, Andrew Palmer and Camila Maistro Patreze review the fungi, such as Trichoderma and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, that could help crops grow in lunar and Martian regolith, and note that none has yet been tested under radiation and other space conditions. [Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspas.2026.1784533)
- **2026-03-05.** Jessica Atkin, Sara Oliveira Pedro dos Santos and colleagues grew chickpeas that flowered and set seed in mixtures of up to 75% lunar regolith simulant, with mycorrhizal fungi on the seeds and vermicompost in the soil; all plants showed stress, and those with fungi lived about two weeks longer. [Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-35759-0)

## Closed life support

- **2026-10-07.** Luke Roberson and colleagues at NASA report in the closeout of the BLiSS project that bioreactors processing crew wastewater on a planetary surface could outperform the physical-chemical systems used on the International Space Station. [NASA Technical Reports Server](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260009398)
- **2026-05-26.** Mattia Esposito, Paola Di Donato and colleagues argue in a review that settlements beyond Earth orbit are most feasible when extracting local resources and recycling through living systems are designed as one loop of shared material and energy flows. [Frontiers in Microbiology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1837116)
