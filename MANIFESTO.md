# Manifesto for a Self-Organizing System Built to Last Millennia

*Working draft 0.4 · October 2026*

## Who we are

We are developing, and testing on ourselves, a way for humanity to organize itself across millennia. Our crews are the first living prototype of that system.

Humanity's move into space will take not decades but thousands of years: at today's speeds, even the nearest star is tens of thousands of years away. No state, company or person lives that long. Only systems that govern themselves and reproduce without a center can last on that timescale.

We are building a cathedral we will never see finished. Cologne Cathedral took more than six hundred years to build, and every stone in it matters. Our stone is a system handed from one generation to the next: the people change, the course holds.

We are the first generation that can leave its home planet. As far as we know, consciousness is rare in the universe, and for now it has a single home. We want it to have many.

## The immutable core

No decision can change these five principles. Everything else in this text can change, under rules the members set themselves. The core is also kept on its own in [CORE.md](CORE.md).

1. **Our course is beyond Earth.** The system exists for humanity's long journey into space, even if no one alive today will see its end.
2. **Every person belongs to themselves.** Everyone is free to join, to disagree and to leave. A person's name, history and data depend on no state, and not on the system either.
3. **No one is left alone in trouble.** A community whose members abandon each other will not survive into the next generation.
4. **Rules are made by those who live by them.** The system has no center that can give orders to crews.
5. **Deeds before promises.** Every promise must be backed before it is spoken aloud.

## How the system grows

The system grows by division, like living tissue, not by orders from above.

- **The crew** is the basic cell: 10–30 people who know each other in person. Any group that accepts this text can found a crew without anyone's permission.
- **Division.** When a crew grows past 30 people, it splits in two. That way everyone stays among people they know by face.
- **Flotillas.** Crews in one city or region join into a flotilla for shared work and for settling disputes. Flotillas link into a single network.
- **Sharing what works.** Every crew may try its own rules within the core. Successful practices are written down and adopted by others; failures remain lessons.
- **Launch.** The founders assemble the first crews. Their special role ends once ten crews exist that were founded without them, or after five years, whichever comes first.

## How the system governs itself

We build on the principles Elinor Ostrom found in self-governing systems that have lasted for centuries, from communal Alpine pastures to the Water Tribunal of Valencia, which by tradition has settled irrigation disputes for more than a thousand years. This research earned her the Nobel Prize in Economics.

| Principle | How it works for us |
| --- | --- |
| Clear boundaries | It is clear who is a member, which crew they belong to and what they are responsible for |
| Rules fit local conditions | Crews in different countries and cultures live in their own way, within the core |
| Rules are made by those who live by them | A crew decides its own affairs; network-wide rules change only with the crews' consent |
| Members monitor themselves | No outside inspectors: decisions and finances are open to everyone |
| Graduated sanctions | First a conversation, then a warning; expulsion is the last step |
| Fast conflict resolution | First within the crew, then the flotilla council |
| Recognized right to self-organize | We operate openly and legally as a nonprofit |
| Nested layers | Crew → flotilla → network; every question is settled at the lowest level that can handle it |

There are no lifetime positions: roles in crews and flotillas are elected and rotate. If any one of us disappeared tomorrow, founders included, the crews would carry on.

## How the system outlasts generations

The system lives as long as it has people, knowledge and shared work. Five mechanisms keep it alive.

1. **Mutual aid.** People stay where they are not abandoned in hard times. This is what keeps a crew from falling apart.
2. **Hand-to-hand transmission.** Japan's Ise Shrine has been taken down and rebuilt every 20 years for more than 1,300 years. It endures not through its wood but because each generation teaches the next. So with us: every skill and every role is passed on to a living apprentice.
3. **Teaching the next generation.** Members' children, and anyone else who wants to, learn physics, engineering and the skills of living in harsh conditions. The system stays alive as long as young people keep joining.
4. **Preserving knowledge.** Science, crafts, experience and personal stories are stored in several places, including in orbit. People beyond Earth will need all of it.
5. **Shared work.** Crews fund and carry out open research and technology for life beyond Earth, and take part in space projects. Without real work, the system turns into a club.

## What we promise each other

We promise only what we can already deliver, and we widen our promises as we grow.

- **You are not alone.** If trouble comes, your crew and the network will help with what they have: people, knowledge, connections. We do not promise to solve every problem, but we promise you will not face it alone.
- **Your contribution will not be lost.** Everything you do for the common work is recorded in a shared ledger that cannot be secretly rewritten.
- **Your identity belongs to you.** Your key and your data stay under your control, and no state can revoke them. Only the community itself can end a membership, under open rules and with the right to a defense.
- **We will look after your memory.** Everyone can leave a personal archive that no one may read before the date they choose. We do not promise eternity, but we will keep it in several places and hand it on to each new generation.

## What we do not promise

- **Immortality.** We do not know what comes after death, and we do not pretend to. We do not sell "digital immortality" or AI resurrection.
- **Passports or protection from the law.** We are not a state and do not claim to be one. If recognition ever comes, it will be a consequence, not a goal.
- **Money.** Membership brings no income. We are not an investment scheme.
- **That you will see the result.** Most likely you won't. Neither will we.

## What we ask of each member

Joining takes effort: that filters out those who only came to look. We ask for nothing beyond this list.

1. Read this text and accept the immutable core.
2. Join through a crew: get recommendations from two of its members, or found a new crew together with others.
3. Give the common work the number of hours a year your crew agrees on: a project, teaching, or helping another member. Money is not a condition of membership.
4. Help others when you can. Saying no carries no penalty.

## Freedom within

A community built to last a thousand years cannot rest on coercion, only on the free choice of each generation.

- **The right to disagree.** You can argue with any decision and any text, including this manifesto. No one is expelled for disagreeing.
- **No required beliefs.** We are united by work, not dogma. Each person's religion, views and private life are their own.
- **Free exit.** You can leave at any time, without explanation. After you leave you owe nothing, and your archive and data leave with you.
- **No isolation.** Membership never requires distancing yourself from family, friends or your former life.
- **Expulsion only in the open.** A person can be expelled only for breaking written rules, with the reasons stated and the right to a defense.

## Shared days and rituals

- **April 12 — Launch Day.** The anniversary of the first human flight into space. On this day crews meet all over the world.
- **Names in flight.** The names of all members fly on every launch where we can secure a place for them.
- **A new crew member.** Joining happens in person, in the presence of those who recommended you.
- **A crew divides.** When a crew splits, both new crews receive the shared history and promise to help each other.
- **Farewell.** When a member dies, the crew gathers. Their name, and their archive if they left one, go into long-term keeping, including in orbit.

*Most likely, we will not see the end of this road. But we will certainly not walk it alone.*
